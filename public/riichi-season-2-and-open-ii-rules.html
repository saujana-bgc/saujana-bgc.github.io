At a glance

Season 2 rewards consistent league play. Open II gives everyone four chances to build a qualifying score before a separate final.

  • League: 1 December 2026 to 28 February 2027. Untimed rated games. Start at 1,500 Elo and earn a rank after eight rated games across four dates.
  • Season winner: in addition to the rank requirement, play at least two rated games from 8–28 February 2027. No rating decay applies.
  • Results: submit by 11:59 pm Malaysia time on the third calendar day after the official gathering. Rated games may start or finish after midnight as part of that gathering and keep its date.
  • Open II: March 2027. Four 90-minute qualifiers, followed by a reset final for the top four. League rank helps set opening tables but does not guarantee a final place.
  • Rules: Riichi Open I gameplay and scoring, including ordinary fives only. No red fives.

Quick glossary

Elo
A rating that rises or falls according to results and opponents’ ratings.
Hanchan
One complete four-player East–South game.
Uma
Placement bonus or deduction added to table-point changes to calculate a hanchan score.
Oka
A first-place bonus used in some formats. This format has no oka.
Chombo
A serious play error handled under the WRC penalty rules. Here its penalty is 30 tournament points.
K-factor
Controls the size of Elo adjustments. Season 2 uses 24. A higher score wins its opponent comparison whatever the margin, and a tie counts half.

Shared gameplay and scoring

Rated league games and Open II use the World Riichi Championship (WRC) Rules 2025, WRC Penalties 2025 and the 29 June 2025 Official Rules Clarification, as adopted by Riichi Open I. The clarification takes precedence where it amends the earlier documents. Saujana’s clock and competition procedures appear below.

  • A hanchan is one four-player East–South game. Each player starts with 30,000 table points.
  • Use ordinary fives in every suit. No red fives or optional WRC variants are used.
  • There is no oka. Play continues if a player has negative table points. Normal dealer continuation applies at the end of South, including when the dealer is in first place.
  • Pause and call the referee for a disputed rule, possible chombo, or disagreement about whether play should continue.

Hanchan scores and penalties

Table points are the points used during play. For the hanchan score, every 1,000 table points gained or lost is worth one tournament point. Then apply uma, the bonus or deduction for finishing position.

Hanchan score before penalties = (final table points − 30,000) ÷ 1,000 + placement uma

Placement uma

1st+15
2nd+5
3rd−5
4th−15

Determine placement from final table points before tournament-point penalties. Players tied on table points share the uma for the places they occupy. For example, two players tied for first each receive +10 uma, and the next player is third. Use the full score in calculations.

Record final table points, unclaimed riichi sticks, uma and tournament-point penalties separately. Unclaimed riichi sticks stay unclaimed. Final table points plus the value of those sticks must total 120,000. All four players confirm the result, and the organiser verifies it before the scoring sticks are reset.

Deduct tournament-point penalties from the score used for Elo or Open standings. A chombo deducts 30 tournament points from the offender’s score under the applicable WRC procedure. For example, a score of +12.4 becomes −17.6 after one chombo. Table points and placement uma stay as recorded.

Riichi League Season 2

  • Season dates. 1 December 2026 through 28 February 2027. The organiser publishes the session schedule before play begins.
  • Starting rating. Everyone starts at 1,500 Elo. Season 2 games determine all Season 2 ratings and eligibility.
  • Rated games. The organiser announces which four-player hanchans are rated before play. Every verified completion counts. Casual play, including three-player games, is also available.
  • League rank. A player earns a rank after at least eight rated hanchans on at least four different dates. Until then the player is provisional, with a rating that updates from their first rated game.
  • Season winner. To be eligible, a player needs the rank requirement above and at least two rated hanchans played from 8–28 February 2027. The highest-rated eligible player wins. Players with exactly equal ratings share their place. Both games may be played on the same date.

Untimed games and interruptions

Season 2 league games have no time limit. Rated games run to their normal conclusion under WRC gameplay rules. Apply the WRC replay procedure if chombo resets a hand.

Before each gathering, the organiser announces the gathering date and the latest date and time a rated game can start, based on the venue closing time and the time a hanchan typically takes. A gathering may continue after midnight. Every rated game that starts before the announced cutoff or is already in progress keeps the announced gathering date. Tables may finish while the venue is open.

If a withdrawal, venue closure or other disruption stops play, the organiser may resume the game within the season with the same four players and a recoverable game state. Void the result if the game cannot meet those conditions or reach an official finish. A void game contributes neither Elo nor eligibility. A resumed game counts on the original gathering date. Its result is due by 11:59 pm Malaysia time on the third calendar day after the game is completed.

Submitting and correcting results

The Season 2 standings on the Riichi League page will show your Elo, the rated games that counted and how many different dates you’ve played. For a missing or incorrect result, contact the league organiser with the date, session and round number, and the score you believe should be recorded.

  1. Use a stable league player ID for each player. Assign each rated hanchan its official gathering date, session and round number when it starts. The recorded round order determines the rating calculation, even if results are submitted in a different order.
  2. Have all four players check the result. One player submits it by 11:59 pm Malaysia time on the third calendar day after the official gathering. Submitting soon after the game helps catch errors, but the full three-day window remains available.
  3. A rated game at an official gathering may continue after midnight while the venue is open. It keeps the gathering date assigned when play began. The submission deadline is calculated from that gathering date.
  4. Submit any corrections by 11:59 pm Malaysia time on 7 March 2027, the seventh calendar day after the season ends. The organiser approves corrections and replays the ratings in the original round order.
  5. Freeze the standings after resolving corrections filed on time. If an Open entrant uses a different name, the organiser confirms which league player ID belongs to them.

For a gathering dated 28 February, the result submission deadline is 11:59 pm Malaysia time on 3 March 2027, including a game that finishes after midnight on 1 March.

The corrections window covers results submitted on time, including results missing from the standings. Ask the organiser to check the original submission. If nobody at the table submitted the result by the three-day deadline, the game does not count towards Elo or rank eligibility.

How Elo changes

Compare a player with each of their three opponents using the official hanchan score after penalties. The actual result for each comparison is 1 for a higher score, 0.5 for a tie and 0 for a lower score.

Expected result = 1 ÷ (1 + 10^((opponent rating − player rating) ÷ 400))

Subtract the expected result from the actual result for each opponent. Average the three differences and multiply by 24. Use the pre-game ratings to calculate all four adjustments, apply the changes together and round only for display. Equal official scores count as ties regardless of starting wind.

Four equally rated players with four distinct official scores change by +12, +4, −4 and −12 Elo from first to fourth. A 1,500-rated player who finishes first against opponents rated 1,700, 1,500 and 1,500 gains approximately +14.1 Elo.

Ratings do not decay during or after the season. A player’s rating changes only when they complete a rated game. Inactivity does not change Elo or rank eligibility. The February activity requirement applies only to eligibility for the Season 2 title. This pilot avoids changing ratings without a game and keeps Open II seeds tied to results; review rating carryover and any decay after the pilot.

Riichi Open II

Open II is scheduled for March 2027, after the final Season 2 standings are frozen. The organiser publishes the event date before registration.

Everyone in the playing field is scheduled for four qualifying hanchans. The four eligible players with the highest combined qualifying scores play one final hanchan. Ranked and unranked entrants follow the same registration and qualification rules. League rank is used for seeding the opening draw and as a check on how the top seeds are spread across the qualifying schedule.

Check-in and no-shows

  1. Freeze Season 2 standings before the event. Publish a check-in cutoff 30 minutes before the scheduled start of Qualifier 1.
  2. Hold seats for confirmed entrants who check in on time. At the cutoff, late arrivals lose their reserved seat. Offer vacancies to checked-in waitlisted players in signup order, then to on-site organiser reserves in the announced order.
  3. Open II needs at least eight players in the playing field, including at least four who are eligible for the final. If the full roster cannot be seated, use the largest field of at least eight that fills tables of four and has at least four final-eligible players. Keep on-time confirmed entrants in confirmation order, then use the published waitlist and reserve order. For example, 15 available players give a field of 12 if no one can fill the last seat.
  4. If you have a confirmed entry, check in on time and the organiser cannot seat you under this fallback, you receive a full refund of any entry fee you paid.
  5. Postpone if fewer than eight players, or fewer than four final-eligible players, can be seated. Publish the fallback and the refund terms before taking entries.

Refunds and rescheduling

  • Confirmed on-time entrant without a seat. The registration organiser contacts you to arrange the full refund. If you need to follow up, use the registration contact and give your entry name and payment reference.
  • Event postponed or qualifiers moved to another day. Your entry carries over. If you can’t attend the new date, tell the registration organiser to withdraw your entry and request a full refund. Certified rounds stay on record and withdrawn entrants are ineligible for the final.
  • Refund timing. The organiser aims to issue the refund within seven calendar days of confirming eligibility and receiving any payment details needed to return it. The organiser will contact you privately if extra details are needed.

Opening draw and starting winds

Draw tables after check-in using the players who will actually play. Order them by eligible league rank, then randomise the order of the provisional and unranked players after the ranked players. Resolve an exact rating tie at a pot boundary with a recorded random draw.

Make four pots. Each pot holds as many players as there are tables. Draw one player from each pot to every opening table. If the roster changes before seating is published, rebuild the pots and redraw if needed. Publication locks the draw. At each table in each round, draw starting East, South, West and North separately using the same random method.

In a four-table field with at least four ranked entrants, the top four seeds start at separate tables. They avoid each other in Qualifier 1 and can meet in Qualifiers 2–4. Everyone starts each game with 30,000 table points, and all four final places are decided by Open results.

Qualifying table rotation

After check-in, the organiser publishes where you’ll sit in all four qualifiers. With the same 16 players throughout, you’ll face 12 different opponents. For a smaller field, the organiser draws a schedule that minimises repeat opponents.

How to read the 16-player rotation

  1. Qualifier 1. Draw opening tables 1–4. Within each table, randomly assign each player one slot from 1 to 4.
  2. Qualifier 2. Your slot number is your table number.
  3. Qualifiers 3 and 4. Find your opening table’s row and your slot’s column. The two numbers in that cell give your table for Qualifier 3 and Qualifier 4, in that order.

Slots are for table assignments. You draw your starting wind separately at each table.

Checking the spread of top seeds

Generate candidate rotations after check-in. Discard rotations that put three or more of the top four checked-in league seeds together at a later table. Compare how many of those seeds each other player faces across all four rounds. Choose randomly among candidates with the smallest gap between the highest and lowest counts. Use the available checked-in ranked players for this check if there are fewer than four.

For example, a draw where some of the other players face one top seed and others face four has a gap of three. A draw where every other player faces three top seeds has a gap of zero. If both draws pass the table checks, the second draw spreads top-seed opponents more evenly.

Withdrawals and reserves

  • Before or between qualifiers. An announced reserve takes the remaining scheduled table places and joins the starting-wind draw. Record the reserve’s scores under their own name. Certified results stay with the original players. Withdrawn entrants and organiser reserves are ineligible for the final. Check opponent variety against the actual participants after a replacement.
  • No reserve after play starts. Hold the next qualifier while looking for a replacement. If fewer than eight players or fewer than four final-eligible players remain, suspend play and seek replacements. If the field still cannot meet both minimums within the venue window, reschedule the remaining qualifiers. Keep all certified results. If the rescheduled field cannot meet the minimums, postpone the event under the published refund terms. Confirm finalists after all four qualifiers are certified.
  • During a game. Stop play at that table, preserve the game state and call the head referee. The referee rules on how play can resume and which result, if any, can be certified. Record the ruling with the round results.
  • Before the final. Offer a vacant seat to the next available eligible player in the certified qualification standings. Use the standard qualification tie-breakers for tied candidates. Their qualification rank still applies in a final tie. Confirm the final roster before drawing starting winds.

Open II’s 90-minute clock

This clock cutoff is a Saujana competition variation to WRC 2025 §10.1, adopted to keep the event schedule manageable. When the signal sounds after dealing has begun, finish that hand and end the game. WRC 2025’s additional-hand timing procedure does not apply to this event.

Use one visible 90-minute clock for every qualifier and the final. Give a warning when 15 minutes remain. For the cutoff, a hand begins when the first tiles are taken from the wall to start dealing.

  • If the signal sounds after dealing has begun, finish that hand and record the result. If chombo resets the hand in progress, replay that hand.
  • If the signal sounds between hands, score the previous hand and end the game.
  • A game can end naturally before the signal. The head referee resolves disputes over whether a hand had begun.

Allow time after each clock window to finish hands, verify results and change tables.

Qualification, tie-breakers and the final

Qualification score = Q1 hanchan score + Q2 hanchan score + Q3 hanchan score + Q4 hanchan score − all qualifying penalties

Use the four hanchan scores before penalties in this formula and count each qualifying penalty once. The four eligible players with the highest qualification scores advance. For an equal qualification score, apply these tie-breakers in order across all four qualifiers, including when determining the order of eligible final replacements.

  1. Most first-place finishes.
  2. Fewest fourth-place finishes.
  3. Highest single-hanchan score before tournament-point penalties.
  4. A secure random order for the players still tied. Record the result.

For the first two tie-breakers, tied players at the highest or lowest table-point total count as first- or fourth-place finishers respectively. Use table points before penalties for these counts.

Finalists draw starting winds and each starts the final with 30,000 table points. The final’s hanchan score after final-stage penalties determines the final ranking and champion. Where final scores are equal, the better qualification rank wins the tie. Remaining entrants are placed in qualification order below the finalists.

For example, an unranked entrant who scores +22, +9, −4 and +6 after penalties finishes with +33 tournament points. If that total puts them third among eligible players, they reach the final and start it at 30,000 table points like all finalists.

Example Open II schedule

This sample schedule starts play at 11:00 am Malaysia time. The first qualifier is followed by a 45-minute meal break. Allow 25 minutes after each later qualifier for finishing hands, checking scores and changing tables. Later start times are targets. If play runs late, keep the 90-minute clock for each round and move the later starts. Awards follow verification of the final result.

When you can leave. Plan to stay until the finalists and standbys are announced, around 7:00 pm. If you’re asked to stand by, the organiser will tell you when you can leave. Finalists should allow time for awards around 8:50 pm and for any delays.

Organiser review and rehearsal

Before registration, rehearse the day-of draw, the 90-minute clock and score certification through the final. Time check-in, table assignments and score verification. Record the last hand completed at each table.

  • Simulate a no-show, a between-round withdrawal with and without a reserve, and a finalist withdrawal with tied replacement candidates.
  • Verify that a reserve gets a separate score record, that certified results survive a suspension, and that the final cut and replacement order use all four qualifiers and the same tie-breakers.
  • The original Open I organiser workflow goes from Qualifier 2 to the final and locks the roster during play. Open II needs support for Qualifiers 3 and 4 and mid-event roster changes before an end-to-end rehearsal.

What to measure after the pilot

Count how many players earn a league rank and how many ranked players check in for the Open. Review late-season participation, changes to the standings, how ranked players are spread across tables, and how often opponents repeat. Use those results to revisit the eligibility threshold, the K-factor and rating carryover into future seasons.

Newcomer ratings

New players start at 1,500 like everyone else. During the pilot, record provisional players’ results and how their ratings move over their first eight games. Check whether early ratings appear systematically too high or low once more games are available. The Season 1 archive is too small and was played under different rules, so it does not justify a newcomer-specific starting rating now.
