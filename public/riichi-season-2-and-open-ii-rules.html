Riichi League Season 2 and Riichi Open II

Season 2 is a three-month league. Everyone starts at 1,500 Elo, and the final league standings seed the opening draw at Riichi Open II. The Open has three qualifiers and a top-four final.

Shared gameplay and scoring

Rated league games and Open II use the World Riichi Championship (WRC) Rules 2025, WRC Penalties 2025 and the 29 June 2025 Official Rules Clarification, as adopted by Riichi Open I. The clarification takes precedence where it amends the earlier documents. Saujana’s clock and competition procedures appear below.

  • A hanchan is one four-player East–South game. Each player starts with 30,000 table points.
  • Use ordinary fives in every suit. No red fives or optional WRC variants are used.
  • There is no oka. Play continues if a player has negative table points. Normal dealer continuation applies at the end of South, including when the dealer is in first place.
  • Pause and call the referee for a disputed rule, possible chombo, or disagreement about whether play should continue.

Hanchan scores and penalties

Hanchan score before penalties = (final table points − 30,000) ÷ 1,000 + placement uma

Placement uma
PlaceUma
1st+15
2nd+5
3rd−5
4th−15

Determine placement from final table points before tournament-point penalties. Players tied on table points share the uma for the places they occupy. For example, two players tied for first each receive +10 uma, and the next player is third. Use the full score in calculations.

Record final table points, unclaimed riichi sticks, uma and tournament-point penalties separately. Unclaimed riichi sticks stay unclaimed. Final table points plus the value of those sticks must total 120,000. All four players confirm the result, and the organiser verifies it before the scoring sticks are reset.

Deduct tournament-point penalties from the score used for Elo or Open standings. A chombo deducts 30 tournament points from the offender’s score under the applicable WRC procedure. For example, a score of +12.4 becomes −17.6 after one chombo. Table points and placement uma stay as recorded.

Riichi League Season 2

  • Season length. Three months. The organiser publishes the dates before play begins.
  • Starting rating. Everyone starts at 1,500 Elo. Season 2 games determine all Season 2 ratings and eligibility.
  • Rated games. The organiser announces which four-player hanchans are rated before play. Every verified completion counts. Casual play, including three-player games, is also available.
  • League rank. A player earns a rank after at least eight rated hanchans on at least four different dates. Until then the player is provisional, with a rating that updates from their first rated game.
  • Season winner. The highest-rated eligible player wins the season. Players with exactly equal ratings share their place.

The league clock and interrupted games

Rated games use a 75-minute clock. If a game is still going at the signal, finish the hand in progress and record the result. A hand reset by chombo still has to be replayed. A verified timed finish counts in full for Elo and eligibility.

If a withdrawal, venue closure or other disruption stops play, the organiser may resume the game within the season with the same four players, a recoverable game state and the remaining clock time. Void the result if the game cannot meet those conditions or reach an official finish. A void game contributes neither Elo nor eligibility. A resumed game counts on the original session date. Its submission deadline runs from completion.

Submitting and correcting results

  1. Use a stable league player ID for each player. Assign each rated hanchan a session and round number when it starts so ratings follow play order.
  2. Have all four players check the result. Submit it by 11:59 pm Malaysia time on the third calendar day after play.
  3. Submit any corrections by 11:59 pm Malaysia time on the seventh calendar day after the season ends. The organiser approves corrections and replays the ratings in round order.
  4. Freeze the standings after resolving corrections filed on time. If an Open entrant uses a different name, the organiser confirms which league player ID belongs to them.

How Elo changes

Compare a player with each of their three opponents using the official hanchan score after penalties. The actual result for each comparison is 1 for a higher score, 0.5 for a tie and 0 for a lower score.

Expected result = 1 ÷ (1 + 10^((opponent rating − player rating) ÷ 400))

Subtract the expected result from the actual result for each opponent. Average the three differences and multiply by 24. Use the pre-game ratings to calculate all four adjustments, apply the changes together and round only for display. Equal official scores count as ties regardless of starting wind.

Four equally rated players with four distinct official scores change by +12, +4, −4 and −12 Elo from first to fourth. A 1,500-rated player who finishes first against opponents rated 1,700, 1,500 and 1,500 gains approximately +14.1 Elo.

Riichi Open II

Everyone in the playing field is scheduled for three qualifying hanchans. The four eligible players with the highest combined qualifying scores play one final hanchan. Ranked and unranked entrants follow the same registration and qualification rules. League rank is used for seeding the opening draw and as a check on how the top seeds are spread across the qualifying schedule.

Check-in and no-shows

  1. Freeze Season 2 standings before the event. Publish a check-in cutoff 30 minutes before the scheduled start of Qualifier 1.
  2. Hold seats for confirmed entrants who check in on time. At the cutoff, late arrivals lose their reserved seat. Offer vacancies to checked-in waitlisted players in signup order, then to on-site organiser reserves in the announced order.
  3. If the last table cannot be filled, use the largest field that fills tables of four and has at least four final-eligible entrants. Keep on-time confirmed entrants in confirmation order, then use the published waitlist and reserve order. For example, 15 available players give a field of 12 if no one can fill the last seat.
  4. Postpone if fewer than four final-eligible entrants can be seated. Publish the fallback and applicable registration and refund terms before taking entries.

Opening draw and starting winds

Draw tables after check-in using the players who will actually play. Order them by eligible league rank, then randomise the order of the provisional and unranked players after the ranked players. Resolve an exact rating tie at a pot boundary with a recorded random draw.

Make four pots. Each pot holds as many players as there are tables. Draw one player from each pot to every opening table. If the roster changes before seating is published, rebuild the pots and redraw if needed. Publication locks the draw. At each table in each round, draw starting East, South, West and North separately using the same random method.

Qualifying table rotation

Publish all three table assignments together before play. With 16 players, every Qualifier 2 table gets one player from each Qualifier 1 table. Every Qualifier 3 table gets one player from each Qualifier 1 table and one from each Qualifier 2 table. This gives each player nine different opponents if the same 16 play all three qualifiers. With a different field size, publish a rotation that minimises repeat opponents.

To build the 16-player rotation, number the opening tables 0 to 3 and randomly assign slots 0 to 3 within each. A player’s slot becomes their Qualifier 2 table number. Add their Qualifier 1 table number and slot number, then take the remainder after dividing by four for their Qualifier 3 table number. The slots are for planning tables. Draw starting winds separately.

Generate candidate rotations after check-in. Discard rotations that put three or more of the top four checked-in league seeds together at a later table. Compare how many of those seeds each other player faces across all three rounds. Choose randomly among candidates with the smallest gap between the highest and lowest counts. Use the available checked-in ranked players for this check if there are fewer than four.

Withdrawals and reserves

  • Before or between qualifiers. An announced reserve takes the remaining scheduled table places and joins the starting-wind draw. Record the reserve’s scores under their own name. Certified results stay with the original players. Withdrawn entrants and organiser reserves are ineligible for the final. Check opponent variety against the actual participants after a replacement.
  • No reserve after play starts. Hold the next qualifier while looking for a replacement. If the venue window runs out before four-player tables can be filled, suspend play and reschedule the remaining qualifiers. Keep all certified results. Confirm finalists after all three qualifiers are certified.
  • During a game. Stop play at that table, preserve the game state and call the head referee. The referee rules on how play can resume and which result, if any, can be certified. Record the ruling with the round results.
  • Before the final. Offer a vacant seat to the next available eligible player in the certified qualification standings. Use the standard qualification tie-breakers for tied candidates. Their qualification rank still applies in a final tie. Confirm the final roster before drawing starting winds.

Open II’s 90-minute clock

Use one visible 90-minute clock for every qualifier and the final. Give a warning when 15 minutes remain. For the cutoff, a hand begins when the first tiles are taken from the wall to start dealing.

  • If the signal sounds after dealing has begun, finish that hand and record the result. If chombo resets the hand in progress, replay that hand.
  • If the signal sounds between hands, score the previous hand and end the game.
  • A game can end naturally before the signal. The head referee resolves disputes over whether a hand had begun.

Allow time after each clock window to finish hands, verify results and change tables.

Qualification, tie-breakers and the final

Qualification score = Q1 hanchan score + Q2 hanchan score + Q3 hanchan score − all qualifying penalties

Use the three hanchan scores before penalties in this formula and count each qualifying penalty once. The four eligible players with the highest qualification scores advance. For an equal qualification score, apply these tie-breakers in order across all three qualifiers, including when determining the order of eligible final replacements:

  1. Most first-place finishes.
  2. Fewest fourth-place finishes.
  3. Highest single-hanchan score before tournament-point penalties.
  4. A secure random order for the players still tied. Record the result.

For the first two tie-breakers, tied players at the highest or lowest table-point total count as first- or fourth-place finishers respectively. Use table points before penalties for these counts.

Finalists draw starting winds and each starts the final with 30,000 table points. The final’s hanchan score after final-stage penalties determines the final ranking and champion. Where final scores are equal, the better qualification rank wins the tie. Remaining entrants are placed in qualification order below the finalists.

For example, an unranked entrant who scores +22, +9 and −4 after penalties finishes with +27 tournament points. If that total puts them third among eligible players, they reach the final and start it at 30,000 table points like all finalists.

Example Open II schedule

This sample schedule starts play at 1:00 pm Malaysia time. It allows 25 minutes after each of the first two clocks for finishing hands, checking scores and changing tables. Later start times are targets. If play runs late, keep the 90-minute clock for each round and move the later starts. Awards follow verification of the final result.

Organiser review and rehearsal

Before registration, rehearse the day-of draw, the 90-minute clock and score certification through the final. Time check-in, table assignments and score verification. Record the last hand completed at each table.

  • Simulate a no-show, a between-round withdrawal with and without a reserve, and a finalist withdrawal with tied replacement candidates.
  • Verify that a reserve gets a separate score record, that certified results survive a suspension, and that the final cut and replacement order use all three qualifiers and the same tie-breakers.
  • The original Open I organiser workflow goes from Qualifier 2 to the final and locks the roster during play. Open II needs support for Qualifier 3 and mid-event roster changes before an end-to-end rehearsal.

What to measure after the pilot

Count how many players earn a league rank and how many ranked players check in for the Open. Review late-season participation, changes to the standings, how ranked players are spread across tables, and how often opponents repeat. Use those results to revisit the eligibility threshold, the K-factor and rating carryover into future seasons.
