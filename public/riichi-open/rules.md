# Four-player rules

These are Saujana’s shared four-player riichi settings for casual tables and tournaments, based on WRC Rules 2025 and the 29 June 2025 official clarification. Riichi Open tournament rules and formal penalties are listed below.

## 1. Basic settings

- Play East–South, from East 1 through South 4. A tournament may set its own event format below.
- Each player starts with 30,000 points.
- Play continues if a player’s score goes below zero. Bankruptcy does not end a hanchan.
- Open tanyao is allowed.
- Atozuke is allowed.
- Use four ordinary fives in each suit. Red fives are not used.
- Dora, ura-dora, kan-dora and kan-ura-dora are used.
- Ippatsu is used.
- Kuikae (swap-calling) is forbidden, including discarding the same tile as the called tile or the tile at the other end of a called sequence.
- A valid yaku is required to win.
- Dora does not count as a yaku by itself.

---

## 2. Calls and draws

WRC chi, pon, kan, riichi, tsumo and ron procedures apply. A discard can be claimed until the next player draws. Once the next player touches the wall, that player can no longer claim the discard, even before drawing. Another player's meld call does not remove the opportunity to declare ron with precedence. When the table is unsure whether an action was timely, stop play and call the organiser.

Under WRC Section 8.2, call precedence is **tsumo → ron (resolved by turn order) → first meld caller → pon/kan over chi when simultaneous or timing is unclear**. When two meld calls are clearly separated in time, the first call wins, including when that first call is chi. If the calls are approximately simultaneous, or the table cannot establish which was first, pon or kan takes precedence over chi. Ron takes precedence over either meld call regardless of the meld callers' timing.

Riichi is permitted with fewer than four tiles remaining in the live wall, including no tiles remaining, and with fewer than 1,000 points. **Riichi with no live-wall tiles remaining is expressly permitted by the 29 June 2025 Official Rules Clarification**, which corrects the conflicting printed wording in WRC Section 8.4. Furiten riichi is permitted. A concealed kan after riichi must use the tile just drawn and must not change the hand's structure or waits; a change to yaku alone is permitted.

Only an added kan can be robbed. A concealed kan cannot be robbed, including for kokushi musou.

### Kan dora

Kan-dora is used.

- For every completed kan, draw the replacement tile and reveal the new kan-dora indicator before discarding. Reveal it even if the replacement tile wins by rinshan kaihou.
- Do not reveal a kan-dora indicator for an added kan that is robbed; turn it face down again if it was revealed prematurely.
- A maximum of four kans may be made in a hand. After the fourth kan the hand continues, but a fifth kan is forbidden.

### Abortive draws

Abortive draws are not used. None of the following automatically ends the hand:

- Nine terminals and honours
- Four first-turn wind discards
- Four riichi declarations
- Four kans by more than one player

The last discard of the live wall may be won by ron, but cannot be called for chi, pon or kan. If no one wins by ron, the hand ends in an exhaustive draw.

---

## 3. Head bump

**Head bump (atama hane) applies.**

This is the WRC single-winner rule.

If more than one player declares ron on the same discard, only one player wins.

The winner is the eligible ron declarer encountered first when proceeding from the discarder in normal turn order. Skip the discarder and any players who did not declare a valid ron.

Any other ron declarations on the same discard are disregarded.

There is therefore no double ron or triple ron.

---

## 4. Dealer and match progression

- The dealer continues after winning a hand.
- The dealer continues after an exhaustive draw when tenpai.
- One honba is added after every dealer win or exhaustive draw. Honba resets to zero after a non-dealer win. Each honba adds 300 points to a ron payment or 100 points from each opponent on tsumo. If a tournament chombo requires a replay, retain the honba.
- Riichi sticks remain on the table until collected by the next winner.
- An untimed hanchan ends after South 4 when the dealer does not repeat. If the dealer repeats in South 4, play continues in South 4 until the dealer stops repeating. For a timed tournament hanchan, the WRC Section 10.1 ending procedure below can end play even if the dealer would otherwise repeat. There is no West round.
- Any riichi sticks still on the table when the hanchan ends remain unclaimed. Record them separately; no player receives them.

### Agari-yame

**Agari-yame is not used.** A dealer who wins South 4 must continue even when in 1st place. Normal tenpai continuation also applies after a South 4 exhaustive draw.

### Time limits

**WRC Section 10.1 procedure for other timed tournament hanchans:** unless the event announces a different procedure, at the signal finish the current hand and play one more; if the hand has just ended, play two more hands. Do not play beyond the natural end of the South round. After the required final hand, end the hanchan even if the dealer won or was tenpai and would normally repeat. If a tournament chombo is called after the signal, replay the affected hand. For casual timed games, agree a finish procedure before play and ask the host how to handle a serious mistake.

---

## 5. Exhaustive draws

Standard 3,000-point noten payments apply.

At an exhaustive draw, declarations proceed in current seat order: East, South, West, then North. Reveal tenpai hands and turn noten hands face down. A player who has not declared riichi may choose noten rather than reveal a tenpai hand. A riichi player must reveal their hand; they cannot conceal it by choosing noten. A declaration cannot be changed.

Tenpai does not require a yaku, and a wait remains tenpai even if all completing tiles are unavailable outside the player's hand. A hand requiring a fifth copy of a tile is not tenpai. In tournaments using WRC formal penalties, a dead hand is noten.

The dealer continues only if tenpai.

---

## 6. Hand scoring

Use the WRC yaku list, fu rules and scoring table.

- **Kiriage mangan is used:** 4 han 30 fu and 3 han 60 fu are rounded up to mangan.
- **Nagashi mangan is not used.**
- Thirteen or more han without a yakuman yaku scores **yonbaiman**, equal in points to a single yakuman. It is not an actual yakuman and does not stack with yakuman yaku.
- Multiple distinct yakuman yaku stack. A special wait does not double a yakuman.
- A double-wind pair is worth 2 fu, not 4 fu. Chiitoitsu is always 25 fu.
- Renhou is mangan and does not combine with other yaku or dora; use another scoring interpretation if it pays more.
- Pao applies to:
  - Daisangen
  - Daisuushii
  - Suukantsu
- Under WRC Section 9.1.1, pao applies only when a player feeds the final required triplet/kan for one of these yakuman, **the recipient's other required sets are already visible**, and the recipient subsequently wins with that yakuman. Visible means melded sets or declared concealed kans; triplets concealed in the hand do not count as visible. If the other required sets are still hidden, feeding the final set does not create pao.
- Pao covers only the yakuman fed. On ron, the liable player and the winning-tile discarder split that yakuman's payment equally; if they are the same player, that player pays the full amount. On tsumo, the liable player pays that yakuman's full value. Other yakuman are paid normally.
- Apply WRC Section 11.2.1 for honba: on ron, the winning-tile discarder pays all honba. On tsumo with one yakuman, the liable player pays all honba; with multiple yakuman, each opponent pays their usual 100 points per honba. Rinshan kaihou alone does not create pao.

Keep the winning tile separate so waits, yaku and fu can be verified. When unsure about scoring, consult the official WRC scoring table between hands or call the organiser.

The organiser's ruling is final.

## Mistakes and disputes

Casual games do not use formal warnings, dead-hand penalties, chombo or tournament-point deductions. Correct a minor mistake if the table can restore the game state clearly. If a serious mistake affects the hand, pause play and ask the host whether to correct the situation or replay the hand. Do not deduct points from a player’s score.

Formal tournament penalties are explained in [Riichi Open tournament rules](#riichi-open-tournament-rules) and apply when announced by the event.

---

## 11. Player conduct

Players are expected to maintain good tournament etiquette.

- Calls and win declarations should be made clearly.
- Discards should remain arranged in order.
- Players must not reveal concealed information.
- Players must not intentionally give information about another player's hand.
- Spectators must not comment on active hands.
- No collusion or signalling is permitted.
- Intentional stalling is prohibited.
- Players should allow opponents reasonable time to act.
- Players must not intentionally manipulate, mark or identify tiles.
- Players should treat opponents and organisers respectfully.
- Keep phones and reference materials away while a hand is in progress. Check scoring aids between hands.

---

## 12. Disputes and rulings

For a rules dispute, illegal action, scoring disagreement or disrupted game state, pause play and call the host or tournament organiser. The host settles casual games. The tournament organiser applies the announced event rules and penalties.

The organiser acts as the event's referee and applies the official WRC documents consistently. Players may politely request that a ruling be reviewed before play resumes. The organiser's final ruling governs the event.

---

## 13. Spirit of the event

Riichi Open is a Saujana Board Game Community event.

Players are expected to compete seriously while maintaining a friendly and welcoming atmosphere.

Play to win, respect the table, and enjoy the mahjong.

# Three-player rules (sanma)

These settings apply to Saujana’s three-player riichi tables. Follow the shared rules above wherever they fit, with the changes below. Sanma has its own tile set, draw progression, scoring payments and multiple-ron rule.

## Sanma table settings

- Use East–South rounds, with East 1–3 and South 1–3.
- Remove the 2-man through 8-man tiles, leaving 108 tiles.
- Chi is not allowed. Pon, kan, riichi, tsumo and ron remain available.
- Use four ordinary 5-pin and four ordinary 5-sou. Saujana tables do not use red fives.
- The manzu dora cycle runs directly between 1-man and 9-man: a 1-man indicator points to 9-man, and a 9-man indicator points to 1-man.
- A valid yaku is required to win. Dora does not count as a yaku by itself.

Each player starts with 40,000 points. Play continues below zero. In casual play, use the shared mistakes procedure above. Do not apply a chombo or tournament-point penalty.

## North tiles (nuki-dora)

A player may declare a North tile as nuki-dora during their own turn and set it aside. Draw the replacement from the dead wall, then move the next live-wall tile to the dead wall so it remains at 14 tiles. Extracted North tiles each count as one dora. A player may keep North in their hand as an ordinary honour tile. After riichi, extract only a North drawn that turn. North extraction cancels ippatsu. No player may win by ron on an extracted North. Do not extract North when no live-wall tile remains for replenishing the dead wall.

## Sanma scoring and draws

- Ron uses the normal four-player point formula. The discarder pays the normal ron amount.
- On tsumo, each of the two opponents pays their normal four-player share. The absent fourth player’s share is not compensated.
- Honba payments are 300 points on ron and 100 points from each opponent on tsumo.
- Use the standard 3,000-point noten pool at an exhaustive draw. With one tenpai player, each noten player pays 1,500 points. With two tenpai players, each receives 1,500 points from the sole noten player.
- The dealer continues only when tenpai after an exhaustive draw. Matches normally end after South 3. If the dealer repeats in South 3, continue until the dealer stops repeating.
- Multiple ron is allowed. Each valid winner receives the normal ron payment and the full honba payment. Award riichi sticks to the valid winner closest to the discarder in turn order.
- If the dealer is one of the valid ron winners, the dealer continues and one honba is added. If the dealer does not win, the dealer rotates as usual.
- The leading dealer may end the match after winning the final scheduled South 3 hand, including a hand with multiple valid ron winners.
- Pao uses the three-player tsumo shares. The absent fourth player’s share is not charged. For a single pao yakuman, the liable player pays the full sanma tsumo amount.

Yaku, fu, limits, riichi rules and other gameplay rules follow the four-player guide unless the difference in player count makes them impossible. For mistakes or disputes, pause play and ask the organiser. Sanma league points are based on finishing place and are described on the Riichi League page.

# Riichi Open tournament rules

These rules apply across the Riichi Open series. An event notice may set the format, schedule, seating and qualification procedures for that edition. Those announced arrangements take precedence over series defaults, followed by Saujana’s shared riichi rules and then the cited WRC documents for details not covered here. Formal penalties apply only when announced by the event.

## Starting points and match scoring

- Each four-player tournament hanchan starts at 30,000 points. There is no oka. Play continues below zero.
- Calculate each hanchan score as `(final points − 30,000) ÷ 1,000 + uma`.
- Uma is +15, +5, −5 and −15 for first through fourth place. Players tied on table points share the uma for the places they occupy.
- Unclaimed riichi sticks remain unclaimed. Keep them separate from players’ final table points.
- Record final points, uma, hanchan scores, unclaimed sticks and tournament-point penalties separately. Verify all four scores before resetting the scoring sticks.

## Qualification and final

Each event notice states the number of qualifying hanchans, eligibility requirements, qualification tie-breakers, final roster and final tie-break procedure. Unless an event notice says otherwise, qualifying scores determine the standings, and finalists begin the final on equal terms at 30,000 points. Tournament-point penalties apply to the stage where they occurred and do not change table points or uma.

## Formal tournament penalties

WRC warnings, dead hands, chombo and tournament-point penalties apply only when announced by the event. Casual games do not use formal penalties. For a chombo, follow the WRC procedure and the announced tournament-point deduction. The event notice states how penalties affect qualification and final scores. Penalties change tournament points, not table points or uma.

Riichi Open follows WRC restrictions on electronic devices, foreign objects and table conversation. Permitted reference sheets and simple calculators may be consulted between hands, not during play.

## 7. Chombo

A serious rules violation may result in a **chombo**.

A chombo carries a penalty of:

**−30.0 tournament points**

Record each chombo separately under WRC Penalties 2025. Penalties stack, are deducted from the offending player's tournament score, and are not transferred to other players. Table points and placement uma are unchanged. Apply penalties to the stage and standings under the announced event format.

For a final, deduct the announced penalty from the player's final tournament score. Do not deduct from table points or recalculate uma. Apply other WRC tournament-point penalties to the stage specified by the event.

### Example

A player finishes with a hanchan score of **+12.4** and receives one chombo penalty:

`+12.4 − 30.0 = −17.6`

Their recorded score for that hanchan after the penalty is **−17.6**.

Chombo penalties do not award additional tournament points to the other players.

---

## 8. Situations that may result in chombo

Use the official WRC penalties document, as amended by the 29 June clarification, to determine the penalty for each incident. Examples of chombo include:

- Declaring an invalid win, including an empty winning declaration.
- Declaring riichi while not in tenpai, discovered at a win declaration or exhaustive draw.
- Making an invalid meld or false concealed kan and then discarding, under the June clarification.
- Committing an illegal action that makes continuation of the hand impossible.
- Seriously disrupting the wall or game state such that the hand cannot reasonably continue.
- Other incidents for which WRC specifies chombo, such as an incorrect tile count or sufficiently serious tile exposure.

Not every mistake results in chombo.

An incorrect tenpai declaration alone normally receives a warning, with a 5-point tournament penalty for repeated offences under WRC; do not automatically treat it as chombo. An invalid meld or false concealed kan before discarding receives a dead hand under the June clarification. Intentional rule violations are cheating. A player caught cheating is immediately disqualified.

The tournament organiser determines whether an incident warrants:

- no penalty,
- a warning,
- a tournament-point penalty,
- a dead hand,
- a replayed hand,
- chombo, or
- disqualification.

A chombo normally cancels and replays the current hand under [Procedure after chombo](#9-procedure-after-chombo). If it occurs simultaneously with a valid win, WRC voids the chombo penalty: score the win normally and warn the offending player. If it occurs simultaneously with an exhaustive draw, apply the chombo and replay without noten payments. Apply the WRC penalty appropriate to the facts; do not replace prescribed penalties with a general exemption for honest mistakes.

The organiser's decision is final.

---

## 9. Procedure after chombo

When a chombo is applied (subject to the simultaneous-win exception under [Situations that may result in chombo](#8-situations-that-may-result-in-chombo)):

- The hand is void.
- The hand is replayed.
- The dealer does not advance.
- Any honba already in play is retained, and no additional honba is added because of the chombo.
- Riichi sticks declared during the voided hand are returned to their owners; sticks already on the table before that hand remain there.
- Record the tournament-point penalty announced by the event and apply it to the relevant stage. Do not change table points or uma.
- If several players commit chombo simultaneously, each receives the penalty; replay the hand once.

---

## 10. Minor mistakes

Use WRC's warning, point-penalty, dead-hand, chombo and disqualification procedures. Correct mistakes only where those procedures permit a correction. A dead hand cannot chi, pon, kan, declare riichi or win, and is noten at an exhaustive draw.

Players should point out errors politely. Whether a mistake was accidental does not remove a penalty prescribed by WRC.

Examples of issues the organiser should assess include:

- accidentally exposing a tile,
- making an incorrect or cancelled call,
- accidentally disturbing tiles without destroying the game state,
- scoring calculation mistakes,
- procedural mistakes that have not materially affected play.

Players should immediately inform the table or organiser when an irregularity occurs rather than attempting to conceal it.
