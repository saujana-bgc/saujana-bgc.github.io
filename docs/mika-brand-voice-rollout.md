# Saujana brand voice rollout

Mika is the editorial reference for Saujana’s voice. On the site, the speaker is simply Saujana. Use “we” for what the community does and “you” for what a visitor can try. Let the personality come through in specific offers of help, curiosity about the games and a little humour where it fits. Keep Mika’s name and narrator framing out of public copy, including image descriptions and search snippets.

The current site already has lines worth keeping. “Find a gathering” tells visitors exactly where the link goes. “Watch a hand or try a turn with help” removes a real worry for someone new to riichi. “Checking the calendar…” sounds natural and reports what’s happening. The opportunity is to write more lines like these and cut explanations that appear twice, such as the duplicate-clue rule on Board Games and the public-list disclosure in Gatherings sign-up. ## Rollout order

1. **Start with Home and Gatherings.** Give the home intro one short invitation, one concrete reassurance for first-timers and a clear link to dates. Keep the activity mix accurate. Board games and riichi mahjong are part of every gathering, with Blood on the Clocktower added on dedicated dates. Let the event card handle logistics. Keep the venue, spend terms, what appears publicly and the RSVP action near the form. Write the loading, no-date, unavailable, error and success messages together so they read like one conversation.
2. **Give each activity a different opening.** Let Board Games open with a small puzzle, Riichi Mahjong with a decision about a tile, and Clocktower with a little suspicion. Follow each with one practical way to join. In Clocktower recaps, keep the playfulness in the introduction and make spoiler and photo controls explicit. Let Birthday Club be affectionate in its invitation and thank-you, with plain wording beside the greeting choice. Describe what’s happening in informative illustrations without naming Mika. Keep decorative images silent for screen readers. 3. **Apply the voice to everyday controls and feedback.** Read the menu, footer, Collection, Play History and the missing-page fallback as a visitor trying to get something done. Use direct labels such as “Find a gathering”, “Sign up” and “Try again”. Give empty searches a useful next step. Keep “No plays logged” wherever the site only knows what has been recorded. Put the warmth in the help, confirmation and recovery copy. 4. **Review high stakes wording against the actual state.** For Riichi Open, distinguish a temporary reservation, a received receipt and a confirmed place. For the League and Calculator, keep scoring terms and instructions exact and use friendly language when guiding a correction. For organiser screens, use direct task labels and clear save or publish outcomes. Keep the approved wording and meaning of rules, consent, deadlines, amounts and results. Give event editors a short description pattern and apply it to new listings. Bring existing published descriptions back for editorial review only where a rewrite is requested. Review page titles, metadata and share previews alongside their pages. Keep searchable names such as Saujana Board Game Community, Riichi Mahjong and Blood on the Clocktower where they tell readers what to expect. ## Ready to publish when

- Read the Home → Gatherings → Sign up journey aloud. It should sound like one host. Each page should have one main action, and a first-time visitor should be able to explain what they can do at the gathering and what signing up records.
- Review the rendered copy on a phone, including form errors and longer names. Read headings, link labels and alt text in isolation to check they still make sense. Remove repeated offers of help, forced jokes and extra copy added only to express the character.
- Check the words against the underlying states. An empty calendar, a failed request and a loading calendar should be distinguishable. A saved birthday, a public greeting preference, a received receipt and a confirmed place each need their own accurate wording.
- Ask a couple of newcomers to find a gathering and tell you what to expect on arrival. Ask a regular to find a league result or use the Calculator. Their ability to complete the task matters more than recognising a particular voice.
